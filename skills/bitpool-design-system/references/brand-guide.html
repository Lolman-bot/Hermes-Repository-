The system in one screen

Five rules that make this Bitpool. Everything else builds on them.

The brand survives because these are absolute. Bend any of them and the result stops looking like Bitpool — it starts looking like every other AI/tech company on the internet.

01
One font: Inter
Inter only, full weight range. No serif. No display fonts. No multiple typefaces. Body is weight 300; emphasis is weight 700 in cyan.
02
Bold cyan replaces italics
Wherever a designer would reach for italic, Bitpool uses <strong> styled in #00ADEF. There is no italic anywhere. Ever.
03
Cyan punctuates, white states
The accent never carries body copy or backgrounds. It marks the words that matter, the numbers that count, and the moments that earn attention.
04
Photography over generated graphics
Cinematic cityscapes carry the storytelling. No wireframe buildings, no particle networks, no neural-network diagrams.
05
Restraint over decoration
Hairline borders. Long slow animations. One idea per section. No HUD chrome, no scanlines, no glitch text, no emojis. The brand is calmer and more architectural than its industry — that is the differentiator.
Foundations · 01

Logo

The Bitpool cube. A single, unmistakable mark. The only visual identifier of the brand.

Bitpool

Usage rules

Clear space

Allow at least the height of one cube face on every side. Never crowd the logo with adjacent text or graphics.

Minimum size

22px on screen. Below that, the cube faces lose definition and the mark stops reading as a cube.

Don't modify

Never recolour, rotate, distort, or apply effects. Always use the master file. The wordmark "Bitpool" is set in Inter Medium, never substituted.

The system · 01

The bold-cyan rule

The single most important typographic decision in the system. Read this twice.

Where most brands reach for italic to add tonal lift to a word, Bitpool uses weight + colour instead. Bold cyan does what italic does in editorial design — it says "this word matters" — but it says it differently. Where italic feels reflective, contemplative, literary, bold cyan feels confident, declarative, engineered. That fits Bitpool's "boldly practical and unapologetically real" personality far better than any italic ever could.

We help buildings run themselves.

Every headline becomes a rhythm

Buildings weren't built to think. Until now.
Boldly practical & unapologetically real.
Tomorrow's autonomy, shipping today.
Trusted building intelligence.
Stop dumb decisions. Prevent waste.

The contrast you're never allowed to use

For comparison only — this is what other brands do. We do not.

Wrong
We help buildings run themselves.
Right
We help buildings run themselves.

The mechanics

One CSS rule, applied globally:

strong, .accent {
  color: var(--accent);       /* #00ADEF */
  font-weight: 700;
  font-style: normal;          /* explicitly kill any inherited italic */
}

Then in markup:

<h1>We help <strong>buildings</strong> run themselves.</h1>

How to choose what to bold

  • Every headline gets one or two emphasis words. Never more — the contrast disappears.
  • Emphasize the word that carries the meaning of the sentence. Not just any word that "feels important."
  • Don't bold the obvious noun. "<strong>Bitpool</strong> helps buildings run themselves" is wrong — the subject doesn't need emphasis. Bold the verb or action: "helps buildings <strong>run themselves</strong>".
  • Don't bold abstract qualifiers like "truly innovative" or "cutting-edge." Find the concrete claim.
  • Don't bold body copy. Emphasis lives in headlines, eyebrows, metric values, and card payoff lines. If a body sentence needs emphasis, the sentence needs to be rewritten.
If you find yourself reaching for font-style: italic anywhere — stop. The answer is bold cyan. There are zero exceptions to this in the brand.
The system · 02

Voice & copy

Boldly practical and unapologetically real. The brand's stated personality is also its writing brief.

Confident, not preachy

State things. Don't sell them. The brand earns trust by being declarative — not by performing enthusiasm.

Wrong
Imagine if your building could run itself!
Right
Buildings that run themselves.
Wrong
We believe that every building decision is critically important.
Right
Every building decision matters.

Specific, not vague

Concrete numbers, named places, real partners. Vague claims read as marketing fluff.

Wrong
Skills shortage looming in our industry.
Right
50% of building engineers retiring by 2030.

Active, not corporate

Verbs that do something. Avoid the SaaS thesaurus.

Wrong
Empower smarter decision-making across your portfolio.
Right
Stop dumb decisions. Prevent waste.

Words to avoid

If a word from this list shows up in a draft, rewrite the sentence with a more specific verb or noun. The rewrite is almost always shorter and sharper.

Banned

leverage empower transform revolutionize unlock harness holistic synergy best-in-class world-class cutting-edge next-generation robust seamless journey innovate mission-critical value proposition thought leadership

The system · 03

Motion

Slow. Confident. Architectural. Motion in Bitpool feels like a building reveal — never bouncy, never frantic.

Reveal-on-scroll

Sections fade up over 1.4–1.8s as they cross 15% into the viewport. Curve: cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.8, 0.2, 1).

Pulse

Live indicators (nav status, partner-row dots, hero tag) pulse on a 1.6–2s cycle with a soft box-shadow glow.

Ken Burns

Photographic backdrops drift and zoom over 40–50 second cycles. Different speeds and directions per photo so the page never feels static.

scale(1) → scale(1.06) translate(-1%, -0.5%)

Sun-breathe

Radial cyan glows oscillate gently in opacity over 10–14s. A nod to the building being alive without being literal about it.

Never use bouncy easing like cubic-bezier(0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1). Never use durations under 1 second for major reveals. The brand's confidence is partly a function of pace.
Components · 01

Axiom cards

For declaring principles, values, or pillars. Five-up on desktop, two-up on tablet. Each card holds an icon, a kicker, a title, and a payoff.

01
Who we are
The AI platform for autonomous building intelligence.
We help buildings run themselves.
02
Why we exist
Stop dumb decisions. Prevent waste.
Every building decision matters.

Anatomy

  • Icon — a 64×64 line-art SVG using stroke="currentColor" so it picks up the cyan from the parent. Each icon is a tiny diagram of the idea, not a generic stock symbol.
  • Number (top-right) — subtle index, weight 300, in --ink-50.
  • Kicker — 10px uppercase eyebrow in cyan, weight 600.
  • Title — 1.35rem weight 400 with one or two cyan-bolded emphasis words.
  • Payoff — anchored to the bottom by margin-bottom: auto on the title, separated by a hairline. Almost entirely cyan-bolded.

Hover state

A 1px cyan line slides in across the top edge over 0.6s. A subtle radial gradient lights up the upper-left corner. The card background shifts from --bg to --bg-2. Three small cues compounding — never a single dramatic shift.

Components · 02

Growth pillars

For strategy, capabilities, or roadmap items. Similar grid to axioms but with body copy and a metric instead of a payoff line.

01

Partners

Extend our alliance of intelligence-driven service providers across Asia, continental Europe, UK deepening, and Canada — layered onto our existing footprint.

+3 regions Expansion · 2026 — 2027
02

Revenue

Aggressive ARR growth through the partner channel and enterprise wins. When partners win, we win.

$2.5M ARR · By 2027

Anatomy

  • Index — same as axiom card.
  • Title — 1.85rem weight 500 (heavier than axiom titles because the body copy below carries less typographic weight).
  • Body — 25–40 words. Concrete, specific, no abstract qualifiers. Set with flex: 1 so the metric pins to the bottom regardless of length.
  • Metric value — weight 700 in cyan, letter-spacing: -0.035em. The single biggest hit of cyan in the card.
  • Metric label — 10px uppercase, ink-50.

See. Know. Act. Grow. The brand's commitment compressed to four words. A four-cell grid framed by stronger borders, with each word entirely in cyan.

Components · 05

Photographic sections

Cinematic cityscapes are the visual backbone of the brand. Three moments punctuate a typical page: hero, mid-page vision, and dawn closing.

Strategic Directive · 2026 / 2027

We help buildings run themselves.

Schematic only — actual hero uses a real photograph (Brisbane at blue hour, in the strategic directive build).

Three roles, three crops

  1. Hero — wide establishing shot. Heaviest left-side darkening so the headline reads cleanly.
  2. Vision (mid-page) — tighter crop, deeper saturation, slightly darker. The "deep night" moment that lands somewhere around the middle of the document.
  3. Closing — more sky, lighter grade. Pairs with the final beat (e.g., "Tomorrow's autonomy, shipping today.").

Treatment rules

  • Always real photography. Never generated cityscapes, wireframe buildings, particle networks, or HUD overlays.
  • Always with a Ken Burns drift — 40–50s cycles, different speeds per photo so the document never feels static.
  • Always an atmospheric overlay — directional gradient (heavier on the side where text sits) plus a top-and-bottom darken for legibility.
  • Always a quiet cyan glow from below or behind — radial gradient with 5–12% opacity to harmonise the photo with the brand palette.
  • Always Brisbane first. The brand's HQ city is the default cityscape. Other cities only appear when the section is specifically about global expansion.
Practice · 01

Page composition

A typical Bitpool page reads like a film. One beat per section, with full-bleed photographic moments punctuating the prose.

01
Hero photo
Full-bleed photo, single headline, single sub-copy line. The opening statement.
02
Problem stats
Animated big numbers that count up from zero. Three or four. Land the "why this matters."
03
Mission
Oversized headline, sub-copy, plus a bold-cyan kicker line.
04
Axiom cards
Five-up grid with custom SVG icons. Principles, values, or differentiators.
05
Vision photo
Mid-page full-bleed cityscape. The cinematic punctuation.
06
Scale
Compact 4-cell stat grid showing where you are today.
07
Values
Five-up cells (no icons, lighter than axioms). Cultural beats.
08
Personality
A single oversized statement, centered. The brand's quotable moment.
09
Globe + partners
Animated SVG world map paired with active and planned partner lists.
10
Growth pillars
Five-up grid with body and metric. The plan or roadmap.
11
SKA promise
The four-word commitment in framed cells.
12
Closing photo
Full-bleed dawn cityscape. The final beat — book-ends the hero.
You don't need every section in every page. The rhythm matters more than the count — solid section, photo, solid section, photo. That alternation is what gives Bitpool documents their pacing.
Practice · 02

Anti-patterns

Things that look like Bitpool but aren't. If you find yourself doing any of these — stop and re-read the system.

Wrong
Italic serif for emphasis
Reaching for Instrument Serif, Playfair, or any italic when a word needs to "feel important." This is the single most common drift.
Right
Bold cyan, weight 700, no italic
Wrap the word in <strong>. The global CSS handles the rest. The bold-cyan rule is non-negotiable.
Wrong
Generated SVG cityscapes & wireframe buildings
Procedural building shapes, neural-network diagrams, particle clouds. They look "techy" but feel demo-y.
Right
Real cinematic photography
Brisbane at blue hour, slow Ken Burns, atmospheric overlays. The brand earns its quietness by trusting reality.
Wrong
HUD overlays, scanlines, glitch text, boot sequences
Corner brackets, latitude/longitude readouts, terminal-style init screens, character-decode animations. Cyberpunk command-center.
Right
Restraint. Negative space. Long slow reveals.
The brand is calmer and more architectural than its industry. That's the differentiator.
Wrong
A second accent colour
Adding green for "good," red for "bad," purple for "premium," etc. Tempting when something needs to "pop."
Right
White / cyan / dark navy. That's it.
If a state needs differentiation, use weight or opacity, not a new colour. The monochrome palette is the brand.
Wrong
Bouncy or staggered-bouncy easing
cubic-bezier(0.34, 1.56, 0.64, 1) or any overshoot. Sub-second durations on major reveals.
Right
Long slow ease-out
cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.8, 0.2, 1) over 1.4–1.8s. The pace is part of the personality.
Wrong
Solid white borders or dividers
border: 1px solid #fff or any pure-white line.
Right
Hairlines at 8% opacity
rgba(255,255,255,0.08) for default, 0.14 for emphasis. Always translucent.
Wrong
Multiple fonts
Display + body + mono. Or Inter + a serif "for elegance." Or a custom display face for "personality."
Right
Inter only
Full weight range. The whole personality range comes from weight + colour, not from typeface variety.
